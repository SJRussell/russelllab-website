Prospective Trainees

Join the Lab

Applications are read twice a year, through one form. This page explains who can apply, what to prepare, which scholarships to apply for, and what we expect of the people who join.

Next application window
February 1 to February 28, 2027

Windows repeat each year, in October and February. The form opens on February 1 and closes at the end of February 28. Applications are read in March and every applicant receives a reply. Late applications wait for the next window. Please do not email application materials, because email applications are not read.

What we look for

These apply to undergraduate applicants. Graduate applicants should meet the same expectations in skills and add their program's own admission requirements.

  • Grades. A Laurier cumulative GPA of 10.0 or higher (A-) on the 12-point scale.
  • Courses. Cell biology and molecular biology are suggested before you apply. My course HE440 Reproduction and Fertility is also suggested.
  • Computation. If you want a computational project, work through the first few modules of the Bioinformatics Crash Course before you apply and say which ones you finished.
  • AI agents. You should be comfortable working with AI agents and know how they work: how a language model produces text, what a harness is (the software that lets a model read files, run commands and call tools), and where these systems fail. The lab uses agents for code and analysis every day.
  • Wet-lab experience. Demonstrated work in a wet lab is a large advantage. Describe what you did yourself.

You do not need to arrive as a reproductive biologist. We look for curiosity about early human development, care with data and records, and willingness to learn across wet-lab and computational work.

What to prepare

The form asks for these items.

  1. Your name, student number, program, year and the stream you are applying to.
  2. An unofficial transcript (PDF).
  3. A CV or resume of two pages at most (PDF).
  4. Short answers, 150 to 200 words each, in your own words: why this lab, naming one project or paper from this site; one piece of work you did yourself and are proud of; a time something failed and what you changed.
  5. A self-rating of your experience with cell culture, qPCR, R, Python, the command line and statistics, and the bioinformatics modules you completed.
  6. The hours per week you can give, and the commitments that compete for them.
  7. Two references (name, role and email). Letters are requested only from shortlisted applicants.
  8. For thesis, directed studies and graduate applicants: a one-page statement of the question you would like to work on and how you would start.

Write it yourself. I do not read emails, cover letters or form answers written by an AI tool. If your answers read as generated, I stop reading and the application is closed. Spell check and grammar check are fine. I want to hear how you think, including the rough edges. In the lab we use AI tools for code and analysis and teach how to do that carefully and openly, but the application is where I need to hear from you.

Several of these deadlines fall before our February window. Apply to the awards that suit you on their own schedule, whether or not you apply to the lab. Dates below come from the official pages as of October 2026. Where a 2027 date is not yet published, the table shows the most recent cycle. Always check the linked page for the current call.

What we expect from the team

  • Show up. Come to the weekly lab meeting and give a short update every two weeks. Tell the team before you are absent.
  • Report in writing. Send a short written update each week: what you did, what is stuck, what comes next.
  • Ask early. If you are stuck for more than two hours, ask a lab member. If it is still stuck, ask me.
  • Document everything. Keep an electronic notebook that someone else can follow and code that runs from a clean start. Results that cannot be reproduced do not count.
  • Tell us about mistakes right away. A mistake caught in a day costs little. One found in a year can cost a paper.
  • Respect the people behind the data. Human embryo and patient-derived data come with consent and ethics rules (REB and TCPS 2). Follow them every time, including on your own laptop. Complete the TCPS 2 CORE-2022 tutorial when you join.
  • Disclose your use of AI. Tell us when an AI tool helped with code, analysis or writing, and check every output yourself. You are responsible for what you hand in.
  • Share credit fairly. Authorship follows contribution (ICMJE criteria), is discussed early, and is revised as the work changes.

I write strong reference letters for people who met these expectations.

If you want to be considered

Step 1

Read the research and projects pages and two lab publications. Pick one project you would want to work on.

Step 2

Check the requirements above. If you are not there yet, use the months before the window to close the gap: bioinformatics modules, a stats or programming course, wet-lab experience.

Step 3

Write your short answers in a plain document first and read them aloud. Gather your transcript, CV and references.

Step 4

Submit the form between February 1 and February 28, and apply separately to the awards that suit you.
